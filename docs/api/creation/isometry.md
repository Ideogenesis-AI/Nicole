# isometry

Build the 3rd-order tensor that fuses two indices into one. Its first two indices match the inputs and its third carries the fused sectors generated by the group's fusion rule. Contracting a tensor against an isometry merges the two corresponding axes into a single axis, and contracting against the conjugate isometry splits them apart again.

::: nicole.isometry
    options:
      show_source: false
      heading_level: 2

## Description

The sectors of the fused index come from applying the group's fusion rule to every pair of input charges: addition for U(1), XOR for Z(2), triangle-rule coupling for SU(2). A charge pair `(q₁, q₂)` fuses to `group.fuse_unique(q₁, q₂)` and contributes the product of its two sector dimensions, accumulated over all pairs that land on the same fused charge. Contributions are direction-aware, so an IN index enters through its dual charge.

Both inputs must share a symmetry group; a mismatch raises `ValueError`. The fused index takes the dual direction of `first` unless `fused_direction` says otherwise, which is the handle for orienting the isometry toward whichever side it will contract against.

For an Abelian group each admissible charge pair produces a single block. Under a generic group one pair can couple through several fusion channels, so a pair contributes several blocks, and the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients along with their normalization are carried by each block's `Bridge`.

## Structure

- **Indices 1 & 2**: the input indices, with directions reversed so they contract naturally against the tensor being fused.
- **Index 3**: the fused index, whose sectors are the fusion products described above.

## Notes

- The default `dtype` is `torch.float64`, and `device` defaults to `torch.get_default_device()`. On MPS, `float64` and `complex128` are downgraded automatically.
- All three itags default to `"_init_"`. Pass `itags` as a 3-tuple to label the two inputs and the fused index.
- Fusing three or more indices is a job for [isometry_n](isometry_n.md), which chains pairwise isometries in order of increasing dimension to hold down the intermediate sizes.
